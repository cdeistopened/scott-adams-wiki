# Data Hook Persuasion

Hormozi's content philosophy intersects with Adams's persuasion theory at a specific point: data is the most underrated persuasion tool because it combines three of Adams's strongest techniques simultaneously.

## The Intersection

Hormozi's content principle: lead with a specific number. "I gained 5,000,000 followers" is a hook. "I grew my audience" is not. The number does the work.

Adams's persuasion stack explains WHY the number works, through three overlapping mechanisms:

### 1. Data as Visual Persuasion

Adams rates visual persuasion as the strongest form. A specific number creates a mental image in a way that an abstract claim does not.

- **Abstract (weak):** "We grew significantly" — No picture. No anchor. The audience fills in whatever "significantly" means to them.
- **Data (strong):** "We added 47 customers in 11 days" — The audience sees 47. They see 11 days. The specificity creates a picture.

Adams's test: "If you can't see it, it's not persuasive." Numbers are visible in a way that adjectives are not. "Enormous growth" is invisible. "340%" is visible.

### 2. Data as Anti-Analogy

Adams's framework `analogies-dont-persuade` identifies the failure mechanism: analogies invite the audience to find differences, which feels like winning the argument against you. Data bypasses this circuit entirely.

- **Analogy (invites critique):** "This is like the iPhone launch" — The audience immediately lists ways your situation is NOT like the iPhone launch. Each difference feels like a refutation.
- **Data (bypasses critique):** "This product converted 23% of free users in the first week" — There is nothing to analogize. The number sits there. The audience can question the measurement, but they cannot analogize their way out of a specific fact.

Data hooks are persuasion-safe in a way that analogies never are. You cannot "find differences" with a number the way you can with a comparison.

### 3. Data as Anchoring

Adams identified anchoring as a core persuasion technique: the first number on the table shapes all subsequent evaluation.

Hormozi's data hooks are anchoring devices disguised as content. When you open with "$100M in revenue," every subsequent number in the conversation is evaluated relative to that anchor. When you open with "1.2M followers in 6 months," the audience's frame for "growth" is permanently set at that scale.

The data hook IS the anchor. Most persuaders anchor with abstract claims ("we're the market leader"). Data-hook persuaders anchor with numbers that create visual, analogy-proof, anchor-setting facts.

## The Kill Shot Variant: Data Kill Shots

Adams's kill shot framework requires three properties: visual, sticky, and containing a seed of truth. Data hooks meet all three when used offensively:

- **"Low Energy Jeb"** (Adams's canonical kill shot) — Visual, sticky, seed of truth
- **"Your competitor's churn rate is 47%"** (data kill shot) — Visual (47%), sticky (specific number lodges in memory), seed of truth (it's a real number)

Data kill shots are harder to counter than label kill shots because labels can be reframed ("I'm not low energy, I'm thoughtful") but data can only be disputed with better data. The rhetorical burden of proof shifts entirely to the opponent.

## Constructing Data Hooks for Persuasion

Hormozi's content formula, translated through Adams's persuasion filter:

1. **Find the most surprising true number** in your argument
2. **Lead with it** — before any context, explanation, or framing
3. **Make it visual** — "$340K" is more visual than "a lot of money"
4. **Make it specific** — "47 customers" beats "dozens of customers"
5. **Let it anchor** — the number sets the frame for everything that follows

### Examples

| Weak (Abstract) | Strong (Data Hook) | Why It Works (Adams) |
|-----------------|-------------------|---------------------|
| "Our product saves time" | "Our product saved 14 hours per week for 89% of users" | Visual (14, 89%), anchor-setting, analogy-proof |
| "I have experience" | "I've closed $2.3M in deals in 18 months" | Visual ($2.3M), anchoring, seed of truth |
| "This market is growing" | "This market grew 340% in 9 months" | Visual (340%, 9), anchoring, specific |
| "People love our service" | "NPS is 78. Industry average is 32." | Visual (78 vs 32), anchoring, comparative data doubles the anchor |

## Diagnostic

For any persuasion attempt:
1. Does it lead with a specific number?
2. Is the number visual (can the audience "see" it)?
3. Does the number set an anchor that frames the rest of the conversation?
4. Is the number analogy-proof (a fact, not a comparison)?

If the answer to all four is yes, you have a data-hook persuasion move that combines Adams's three strongest techniques in a single sentence.
